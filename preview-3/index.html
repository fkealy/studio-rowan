Our story

70,000

pairs of disposable slippers. One hotel. One year.

So, where did it all begin?

It was summertime in the Swiss Alps. The moon was shining, and the campfire was crackling. A group of friends sat by a lake, beers in hand, chatting about work. One of them managed a hotel and mentioned, almost in passing, that the hotel got through 70,000 pairs of disposable slippers a year.

70,000 pairs. Used once, often for a matter of minutes, and thrown away. It was one of those numbers that was difficult to forget about.

Once back in the UK, we reached out to hotels and spas and quickly realised that this was not an isolated issue: by our estimate, America alone sends around 120 million slippers to landfill each year.

We thought there had to be a better way.

So we redesigned them.

The Never-Ending Slipper.

A better hotel slipper, designed for a world with less waste.

The conventional hotel slipper isn’t just wasteful. It’s not particularly good at being a slipper.

They offer little support. Limited sizing. They aren’t particularly comfortable. And despite spending much of their (very short) lives around pools and spas, they aren’t even waterproof.

We saw an opportunity to change that.

Why it holds up.

  1. Recycled EVA foamMade from what already exists.
  2. WaterproofBuilt for pools and spas, not in spite of them.
  3. Supportive, wider fitComfortable for a wider range of guests.
  4. Treaded soleDesigned with wet, poolside floors in mind.

Most importantly, they can be washed and used again.

And again.

And again. And again. Every wash keeps another disposable pair out of landfill.

Every wash keeps another disposable pair out of landfill, and brings down the environmental and financial cost.

They’re better for guests. Better for businesses. Better for the planet. And, crucially, better than what came before.

Our mission

We don’t think the world has a waste problem. We think it has design problems.

  • iBut times have changed.
  • iiThe world has changed.
  • iiiAnd it’s important that we change too.

Many of the products and systems we use today were designed decades ago and, at the time, they made sense for the world they existed in.

Our mission is to re-examine the outdated products and systems that create unnecessary waste and design better alternatives. Alternatives that aren’t just better for the planet, but better for people, business and budgets too.

Because we don’t want our products to be better for the planet and as good as they were before. We want them to be better for the planet, and better than they were before.
